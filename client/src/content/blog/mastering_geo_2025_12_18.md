AI models can misrepresent your brand through hallucination, outdated information, or context conflation. When a model incorrectly states your pricing, attributes wrong claims to your company, or confuses you with competitors, brand damage occurs at scale.

Our December 2025 research explores protective strategies for brand safety in the AI discovery landscape.


## The Hallucination Risk

Large language models sometimes generate confident but incorrect information. Your brand can be associated with claims you never made, products you do not sell, or controversies you were not part of.

According to research from [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/), hallucination rates vary by topic, with less common entities experiencing higher rates. Smaller brands are at greater risk than household names.


## Monitoring Requirements

You cannot protect what you do not monitor. Regular queries across AI platforms for your brand name, products, and executives reveal misrepresentations before they spread.

Document problematic responses. Some platforms provide feedback mechanisms. For major issues, direct contact with platform operators may be necessary.


## Authoritative Source Establishment

The stronger your entity presence, the less likely hallucination becomes. Comprehensive, consistent information across trusted sources gives models accurate data to draw from.

Wikipedia articles, LinkedIn profiles, official press releases, and verified social accounts all contribute to the authoritative record that prevents hallucination.


## Correction and Update Strategies

When you discover misrepresentation, the fix is not immediate. Models update on varying schedules. The strategy is strengthening correct information so future model versions get it right.

Publish explicit correction content. Ensure all official sources reflect accurate information. Build the authoritative record that future training will incorporate.
