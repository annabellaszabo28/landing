Most year-end reviews are exercises in confirmation bias. Teams select metrics that show improvement, explain away metrics that declined, and set next year goals based on optimism rather than evidence.

Our December 2025 playbook provides a framework for honest metrics review that prepares you for reality rather than fantasy.


## The Honest Metrics Framework

Start by listing every metric you tracked, not just the ones that improved. For each metric, document the actual result, the comparison to last year, and the comparison to your original goal.

The gap between goal and result is your forecasting accuracy. If you consistently miss by thirty percent, your planning process is broken regardless of whether growth occurred.

Honest assessment of forecasting accuracy is more valuable than celebrating achieved goals because it reveals systematic bias in your planning.


## Leading vs. Lagging Indicator Analysis

Lagging indicators tell you what happened. Leading indicators predict what will happen. Most year-end reviews focus on lagging indicators because they are easier to measure and more satisfying to celebrate.

According to [Measure What Matters](https://www.whatmatters.com/), The leading indicators you choose to track determine what your team pays attention to. Wrong leading indicators create wrong behavior.

Review which leading indicators actually predicted outcomes and which were noise.


## Honest Root Cause Analysis

When something worked, you probably do not know why. When something failed, you probably have a convenient excuse. Neither is useful for planning.

The five whys technique forces deeper examination. Keep asking why until you reach factors you can actually control. If your answer involves luck or external factors, you have not found a useful cause.


## Goal Setting with Safety Margins

Based on your forecasting accuracy, build safety margins into next year goals. If you historically miss by twenty percent, set goals twenty percent below what you actually need to achieve.

This is not lowering standards. It is honest planning that enables confident resource allocation.
