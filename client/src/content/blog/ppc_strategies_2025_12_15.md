Garbage in, garbage out. Without accurate conversion tracking and appropriate attribution modeling, optimization becomes guesswork. Our December 2025 research provides frameworks for measurement that enables improvement.


## The Tracking Foundation

Before discussing attribution, tracking must work. Conversion pixels must fire on actual conversions. Values must be accurate. Deduplication must prevent double counting.

Audit your tracking setup regularly. Platforms change, websites change, and tracking breaks in subtle ways that persist until discovered. Schedule quarterly tracking audits.

According to [Google's measurement documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/), approximately 30% of conversion tracking implementations have significant accuracy issues.


## Attribution Model Selection

Last-click attribution credits the final touchpoint before conversion. It ignores all prior influence. This penalizes top-of-funnel activities that initiate customer journeys.

Data-driven attribution models use machine learning to distribute credit based on actual contribution patterns. They are more accurate but require sufficient conversion volume to work.

Match model complexity to your conversion volume. Simple models for low volume. Complex models for high volume.


## View-Through Conversion Debate

View-through conversions credit impressions that users saw but did not click before later converting. They are real but inflated: the user might have converted anyway.

Include view-through conversions with appropriate weighting. Excluding them entirely misses real influence. Including them at full weight overstates ad impact.


## Cross-Device Challenges

Users research on mobile and convert on desktop. Cookie-based tracking misses this journey. Platform-native tracking handles it better but creates platform-specific blind spots.

Accept imperfect measurement. Use directional signals rather than precise numbers. Compare trends rather than absolute values.
