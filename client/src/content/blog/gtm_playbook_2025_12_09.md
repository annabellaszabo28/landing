Partnerships look attractive because they promise distribution without marketing spend. Someone else's audience, someone else's trust, your product. The reality is more complex.

Most partnerships fail because partnership is easy to agree to and hard to execute. Our December 2025 playbook provides a framework for partnerships that actually generate value.


## Partnership Type Selection

Integration partnerships embed your product within someone else's ecosystem. Channel partnerships use someone else's sales team to sell your product. Co-marketing partnerships share audiences for mutual benefit.

Each type has different requirements and different failure modes. Integration partnerships fail when the technical investment exceeds the customer value. Channel partnerships fail when partner incentives are not aligned. Co-marketing partnerships fail when audience overlap is too low.

Select the partnership type that matches your current capabilities and go-to-market needs.


## The Win-Win-Win Framework

Sustainable partnerships require three parties to win: you, your partner, and the shared customer. If any party loses, the partnership will not last.

According to partnership research from [PartnerStack](https://www.partnerstack.com/), The most common failure mode is focusing on your own win without ensuring partner economics work. Partners will deprioritize relationships that do not generate value for them.

Model the partnership economics from your partner's perspective before proposing anything.


## Integration Depth as Moat

Shallow integrations are easy to build and easy to replace. Deep integrations that embed your product into partner workflows create switching costs that protect the relationship.

The goal is to become infrastructure rather than feature. When your product is a component others build on top of, the relationship becomes structural rather than transactional.
