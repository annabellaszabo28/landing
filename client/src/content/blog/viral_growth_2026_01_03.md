What gets measured gets managed. The metrics you prioritize determine organizational behavior. Our January 2026 research explores how to select north star metrics that drive sustainable growth.


## The North Star Concept

A north star metric is the single primary metric that captures customer value creation. All teams should understand how their work contributes to this metric.

According to growth methodology, north star metrics should capture value for customers, not just value captured from customers. Revenue is a lagging indicator of value. Leading indicators predict revenue through value creation.


## Good vs Bad Metrics

Good north star metrics are: measurable (you can track them), actionable (you can affect them), correlated with long-term value (they predict retention and monetization).

Bad north star metrics are: vanity metrics (easy to increase without value), lagging indicators (respond too slowly), easy to game (optimize without benefit).


## Metric Selection by Model

Different business models suggest different north star metrics. Subscription businesses often use weekly active users or engagement depth. Transactional businesses often use transaction volume or frequency.

The key is capturing the behavior that creates value. If engaged users retain and pay, measure engagement. If transaction frequency predicts LTV, measure transactions.


## Metric Alignment Across Teams

Once the north star is selected, all teams should understand their contribution. Engineering builds features that move the metric. Marketing acquires users who contribute to the metric. Product designs experiences that increase the metric.

Shared metrics create aligned incentives. Siloed metrics create local optimization that damages the whole.
