The single-device customer journey is extinct. Our December 2025 data shows that the average purchase now involves four point three device switches, up from two point seven just eighteen months ago.

Users research on their phone during commute, compare on their work laptop, share with partners on tablet, and complete purchase on desktop. Each switch is an opportunity for dropout.

Traditional analytics that treat each device as a separate user dramatically undercount actual visitors while dramatically overcounting reach.


## The Handoff Problem

Every device switch is a potential journey end. The user gets distracted, forgets where they were, or cannot find their saved state on the new device.

Login walls make this worse. Requiring authentication to save state creates friction that prevents state from being saved at all. Users would rather restart than login.

The alternative is device-agnostic session persistence through email magic links, QR code handoffs, or browser sync integration. Make continuity effortless.


## Cross-Device Design Principles

Each device has different strengths. Mobile excels at quick research and initial discovery. Desktop excels at complex comparison and form completion. Tablet excels at shared decision making.

Design your experience to play to each device's strengths rather than providing identical experiences everywhere. Guide users toward the optimal device for each stage.

According to [Google research](https://www.google.com/), Users who complete purchases on desktop after mobile research convert at three times the rate of mobile-only journeys.


## Attribution Reality Check

Last-click attribution is fiction in a multi-device world. The device that closes the sale is rarely the device that initiated the journey.

Probabilistic cross-device matching helps, but the real solution is moving away from device-level attribution entirely. Focus on household or individual attribution using first-party data strategies.
