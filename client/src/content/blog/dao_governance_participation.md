Most DAOs (Decentralized Autonomous Organizations) are ghost towns.
They have a Discord server, a Snapshot page, and 5% voter participation.
Why? Because governance is usually boring compliance work.
But smart protocols are realizing that **Governance is Marketing**.

## The Stakeholder Economy
When a user holds your token, they aren't just a customer; they are a partial owner.
They want to feel heard.
If you give them meaningful decisions to make, they stick around.
*   **Fee Switch Activation**: Let users vote on when to turn on revenue sharing.
*   **Treasury Allocation**: Let users decide which marketing campaigns to fund.

## Gamifying the Vote
Participation should feel rewarding, not like homework.
*   **POAPs (Proof of Attendance)**: Issue NFT badges for voting on key proposals.
*   **Delegation Battles**: Encourage "political campaigns" where community leaders compete for delegated votes. This creates content and drama (the good kind).

## Avoiding the Oligarchy
The danger of DAO governance is that whales (large holders) control everything.
To mitigate this, explore **Quadratic Voting** or **reputation-based weight**, where active contributors get more say than passive holders.

## Conclusion
A DAO is not a legal structure. It is a community of believers.
Give them a sword and a shield, and they will fight for your brand.
