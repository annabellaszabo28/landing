The most common GTM mistake is treating positioning as a marketing exercise that happens after the product is built. In reality, positioning should drive every product decision from the beginning.

If you cannot articulate why a specific customer segment should choose you over every alternative, including doing nothing, you do not have a positioning problem. You have a product problem.

Our November 2025 playbook focuses on finding the frame that makes you the obvious choice.


## The Positioning Statement Framework

A complete positioning statement answers five questions: Who is the customer? What is the problem? What is the category? What is the differentiator? What evidence proves the differentiation?

Most teams can answer the first three. The struggles begin with differentiation and evidence. Being better is not differentiation because better is subjective and competitors claim it too.

True differentiation comes from being different in a way that matters specifically to your target customer. Speed matters to some segments. Reliability matters to others. Price matters to different segments entirely.


## Category Creation vs. Category Entry

Entering an existing category means accepting existing comparison frameworks. You will be compared feature by feature against established players. This is almost always a losing position for new entrants.

Category creation means defining a new comparison framework where you win by definition. According to the book [Play Bigger](https://www.playbigger.com/), Category creators capture seventy six percent of market value in their space.

The risk is that the category you create might not resonate. The reward is that resonant categories compound permanently.


## The Customer Selection Imperative

Positioning for everyone positions for no one. The strongest market positions come from narrow customer focus that feels almost uncomfortably specific.

When you focus narrowly, you can speak directly to specific pains, reference specific contexts, and become unmistakably relevant to that segment while becoming invisible to everyone else. This is a feature, not a bug.
