Third-party cookies are dying. iOS tracking restrictions limit visibility. Privacy regulations constrain data collection. The targeting capabilities advertisers relied upon are degrading.

Our January 2026 research explores how to adapt paid strategies for the privacy-first era.


## The First-Party Data Imperative

First-party data is data you collect directly from your customers with consent. Unlike third-party data, it is unaffected by privacy restrictions. It is also more accurate and valuable.

According to [Google's privacy documentation](https://www.google.com/), advertisers using first-party data for targeting see 30% better performance than those relying on third-party signals.

Build your first-party data asset: email lists, purchase history, preference data. This becomes your targeting foundation.


## Customer Match Strategies

Customer match allows uploading your customer list for targeting across platforms. This creates high-accuracy audiences unaffected by cookie deprecation.

Build segments from your customer data: high-value customers for upsell, churned customers for win-back, engaged prospects for conversion.


## Conversion API Implementation

Server-side tracking through conversion APIs maintains measurement accuracy when browser-side tracking fails. Events fire from your server rather than the user's browser.

This investment is now required, not optional. Without conversion APIs, measurement degrades and optimization suffers.


## Contextual Targeting Renaissance

With behavioral targeting declining, contextual targeting grows. Placing ads on relevant content pages does not require user tracking.

The sophistication of contextual targeting has increased. Modern systems understand content meaning, not just keywords, enabling effective placement without personal data.
