Most founders underprice their products. They fear that high prices will scare away customers, so they race to the bottom. This is almost always wrong.

Price is a signal. Low price signals low quality, low confidence, or commodity status. Premium price signals value, expertise, and category leadership.

Our November 2025 playbook explores pricing as a strategic lever rather than a simple economic calculation.


## The Value Metric Selection

Your value metric is what you charge for. Monthly subscriptions charge for time. Per seat pricing charges for users. Usage pricing charges for consumption. Transaction pricing charges for outcomes.

The right value metric aligns your revenue with customer value creation. If customers succeed, you succeed. If they do not use the product, they do not pay much.

According to [Price Intelligently research](https://www.priceintelligently.com/), Companies that align their value metric with customer value perception see thirty one percent higher growth rates.


## Anchoring and Decoy Pricing

Humans evaluate prices relative to anchors, not absolutely. A thousand pound product feels expensive alone but reasonable next to a five thousand pound alternative.

The decoy effect works by adding an option that is clearly inferior to make the target option look better. Three tier pricing often uses the middle tier as a decoy to push users toward the highest tier.

These are not tricks. They are acknowledgments that human pricing perception is inherently relative.


## The Freemium Decision

Freemium works when your free tier creates upgrade pressure through natural usage growth. It fails when free users are happy to remain free forever.

The key question: does using your product for free create problems that paying solves? Storage limits create this pressure. Social features with premium badges create this pressure. Simply being nice does not create this pressure.
