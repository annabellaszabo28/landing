Users ask AI assistants questions differently than they type search queries. The shift from keywords to conversation changes which content gets surfaced and how it gets matched.

Our November 2025 research explores how to optimize for the natural language patterns of AI-mediated discovery.


## From Keywords to Questions

Traditional SEO optimized for keywords: short phrases users typed into search boxes. AI queries are full questions with context, nuance, and implicit assumptions.

"Best CRM small business" becomes "What CRM should I use for my 10-person consulting firm that needs integration with Quickbooks?" The query contains much more information about user intent and constraints.

According to [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/research/), conversational queries are on average four times longer than traditional search queries and contain twice as many intent signals.


## Implicit Assumption Matching

Conversational queries contain implicit assumptions about what kind of answer is needed. "How do I..." expects a tutorial. "Why should I..." expects persuasion. "What is the best..." expects comparison.

Content that matches the implicit answer format gets selected over content that technically addresses the topic but mismatches the expected format.


## Question Anticipation Strategy

Effective GEO requires anticipating the questions users will ask and explicitly answering them in your content. FAQ sections, explicitly phrased questions in headings, and question-answer pairs all improve conversational matching.

The heading "Understanding Account Abstraction" matches fewer queries than "What is Account Abstraction and Why Does It Matter?"


## Context Window Considerations

AI models have limited context windows. Long, rambling content may have key information truncated during processing. Concise, front-loaded content performs better for query matching.

Get to the answer quickly. Provide essential context. Elaborate only after establishing the core response.
