Push notification permission rates have fallen to historic lows. Our December 2025 data shows an average opt-in rate of just eleven percent across consumer applications, down from thirty one percent three years ago.

Users have been burned too many times. They granted permission expecting value and received spam. Now the default response to any notification request is immediate denial.

This creates a critical challenge: push notifications remain the most effective re-engagement channel, but accessing it requires rebuilding trust that decades of abuse have destroyed.


## The Permission Wall Problem

Most applications make the same mistake: requesting notification permission immediately on first launch. This is the worst possible timing.

The user has not experienced value yet. They have no reason to believe your notifications will be worthwhile. They have every reason from past experience to assume they will be annoying.

Asking at this moment achieves worst case results. You get a single chance to ask, and you waste it before establishing any relationship.


## Value First Permission Strategy

The alternative is demonstrating value before asking. Let users experience a notification-worthy event without the notification, then show them what they missed.

When a user manually checks for an update and finds one, that is the moment to ask: would you like to be notified next time so you do not have to check? The answer changes from automatic no to considered yes.

This approach requires patience. It means accepting worse metrics for the first week in exchange for dramatically better lifetime engagement.


## Notification Design Best Practices

Even with permission, notification abuse destroys permission. According to [Braze research](https://www.braze.com/), Users who receive more than six notifications per day are sixty percent more likely to disable notifications entirely.

The best notifications are urgent, personal, and actionable. They tell users something they need to know right now and give them a clear next step. Everything else should be email or in-app messaging.
