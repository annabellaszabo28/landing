In the DeFi summer of 2020, "Liquidity Mining" was the rage. You give users free tokens for providing liquidity. It worked—until the rewards ran out. Then the "mercenary capital" moved to the next farm.
In 2026, we focus on **Sticky Liquidity**.

## The Problem with Renting Liquidity
Liquidity mining is essentially paying for users. It is customer acquisition cost (CAC), often paid in equity (tokens). If your LTV (Lifetime Value) is lower than your CAC, you are dying a slow death.
Most protocols failed because they rented liquidity instead of buying loyalty.

## Utility-First Token Design
We help clients design [Web3 Ecosystems](/web3) where the token is necessary for the product to function, not just a reward.
*   **Access**: Staking tokens to unlock premium features or higher data limits.
*   **Governance**: Not just voting, but "curation." Using tokens to signal which assets or content should be promoted.
*   **Work**: Staking tokens to perform work for the network (like a node operator).

## Vesting via Activity
Instead of time-based vesting, we advocate for **Activity-Based Vesting**.
Investors and team members unlock their tokens not just by waiting, but by contributing.
*   Did they vote?
*   Did they run a node?
*   Did they refer a developer?

This aligns incentives. If the protocol grows, everyone wins. If it stagnates, the sell pressure is contained.

## Conclusion
A token is a powerful coordination tool, but it is not a magic wand.
If your product doesn't have product-market fit without the token, adding a token won't fix it. It will just accelerate the failure.
Build a great product first. Then, supercharge it with tokenomics.
