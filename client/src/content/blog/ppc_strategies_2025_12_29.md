Scaling paid acquisition sounds simple: spend more, get more. Reality is harsher. Increased spend often means decreased efficiency as you exhaust your best audiences and compete for increasingly marginal traffic.

Our December 2025 research provides frameworks for scaling while maintaining acceptable economics.


## The Diminishing Returns Reality

Your first thousand pounds reaches your best prospects on your best placements. Your second thousand reaches slightly less ideal prospects. Each additional increment reaches increasingly marginal audiences.

According to [industry benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/), CAC typically increases 15-20% for each doubling of spend. This is manageable if anticipated but devastating if unexpected.


## The Horizontal vs Vertical Decision

Vertical scaling means spending more on existing campaigns. It is faster but hits diminishing returns faster. Horizontal scaling means launching new campaigns targeting new audiences or keywords. It is slower but can maintain efficiency longer.

Profitable scaling usually requires both: vertical scaling on proven campaigns until efficiency degrades, then horizontal expansion to new vectors.


## The Margin Absorption Calculation

As you scale, efficiency decreases. The question is: how much decrease can your business absorb? If your margins allow 200 pound CAC, you have more scaling room than if your margins only allow 100 pound CAC.

Calculate your efficiency floor. Scale until you approach it. Stop or find new vectors before crossing it.


## The Platform Expansion Path

When you max out one platform, expansion to additional platforms provides new audience without competing against yourself. Each platform offers different audience access.

Platform expansion maintains efficiency better than aggressive scaling on a single platform, though it requires operational capacity to manage multiple campaigns.
