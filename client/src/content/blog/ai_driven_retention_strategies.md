Most companies treat churn as a autopsy. "Why did they leave?" is the wrong question.
The right question is: "Who is *about* to leave, and what can we do about it right now?"
In 2026, retention is not about email blasts; it is about predictive behavioral modeling.

## The Lagging Indicator Trap
Traditional metrics like Daily Active Users (DAU) and Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) tell you what happened *yesterday*. They are useless for preventing what will happen *tomorrow*.
If a user stops logging in, it's already too late.

## Predictive Signals
At BlockMarketing, we build custom retention models for our [Product Planning](/services/product-planning) clients. We look for "Micro-Churn Signals":
*   **Feature Degradation**: A user who used to use Feature A 5 times a day now uses it twice.
*   **Ticket Velocity**: A sudden spike in support tickets often precedes churn—or paradoxically, a sudden *silence* after a period of noise.
*   **API Latency Sensitivity**: For technical products, users who experience 200ms of extra latency for 3 consecutive days are 40% more likely to churn.

## The AI Intervention
Once our model flags an "At-Risk" user, we don't just send a generic "We miss you" email.
We deploy **Autonomous Intervention Agents**.
*   **The Pricing Agent**: If the model suggests price sensitivity, the agent offers a dynamic, time-bound discount.
*   **The Support Agent**: If the issue is technical, the agent proactively reaches out: "I noticed your API calls are slower than usual. Can I help optimize your query?"

## Conclusion
Retention is an engineering problem, not a marketing problem.
Stop guessing. Start predicting.
