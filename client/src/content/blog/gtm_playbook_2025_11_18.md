A mediocre product with great distribution will beat a great product with mediocre distribution every time. Our November 2025 analysis confirms that distribution advantage is the single strongest predictor of market success.

Yet most founding teams spend ninety percent of their time on product and ten percent on distribution. This ratio should be reversed in the early stages.

This playbook provides a framework for thinking strategically about channels and building distribution advantages.


## The Channel Selection Matrix

Not every channel works for every product. The key variables are customer acquisition cost, customer lifetime value, and scalability.

High-touch sales works when lifetime value justifies the cost: enterprise software, complex B2B, financial services. Content marketing works when purchase decisions involve research: considered purchases, technical products, professional services.

Paid acquisition works when economics are favorable and creative differentiation is possible. Viral loops work when sharing creates value for the sharer, not just the company.


## Channel Concentration vs. Diversification

Early-stage companies should concentrate. Find one channel that works and exhaust it before diversifying. Spreading thin across many channels means failing at all of them.

According to [First Round Capital research](https://firstround.com/), Successful startups typically get seventy percent of their growth from a single channel in the first two years. Diversification comes later.

The risk of concentration is platform dependency. The reward is focus and depth that generalists cannot match.


## Building Distribution Moats

The best distribution is owned, not rented. Email lists, community platforms, and direct customer relationships are owned. Paid advertising, SEO, and social media are rented.

Owned distribution compounds over time. Rented distribution resets to zero if the platform changes rules or costs increase. Invest in building owned channels even when rented channels are working.
