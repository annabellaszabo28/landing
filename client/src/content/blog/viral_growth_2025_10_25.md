Virality is not luck. It is design. Products that spread do so because they tap into fundamental psychological triggers that motivate sharing. Our October 2025 research explores these triggers and how to incorporate them into product design.


## The Social Currency Trigger

People share things that make them look good. Content that makes the sharer appear smart, informed, or ahead of the curve generates sharing because sharing benefits the sharer.

According to Jonah Berger's research published in [Contagious](https://jonahberger.com/), social currency is the strongest predictor of sharing across categories. People share not for altruism but for self-presentation.

Design experiences that make users feel like insiders. Early access, exclusive information, and discovery opportunities all create shareable moments.


## The Practical Value Trigger

Useful information gets shared because sharing it is helpful. If your product or content solves a real problem, users share it with others who have that problem.

Practical value content includes how-to guides, tool recommendations, and problem solutions. The more specific and actionable, the more shareable.


## The Trigger Exposure Frequency

Products linked to common triggers get remembered and mentioned more frequently. If your product associates with something users encounter daily, they think of you during those encounters.

Rebecca Black's "Friday" is terrible music that went viral because people encounter Friday every week. The trigger exposure frequency drove sharing despite quality issues.


## The Emotional Intensity Requirement

High-arousal emotions drive sharing. Awe, excitement, amusement, and anger all increase sharing. Low-arousal emotions like sadness or contentment do not.

Content that evokes strong reactions spreads. Content that evokes shrugs dies.
