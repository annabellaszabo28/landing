Unlike traditional search with Google's near-monopoly, AI assistance is fragmenting across multiple platforms. Users asking the same question to different assistants get different answers from different sources.

Our December 2025 research explores how to maintain visibility across the multi-model landscape.


## Platform Distribution Reality

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and emerging models each have different training data, knowledge cutoffs, and retrieval mechanisms. Content cited by one platform may be invisible to another.

According to [SimilarWeb data](https://www.similarweb.com/), the AI assistant market is splitting roughly 40-25-20-15 across major platforms, meaning single-platform optimization leaves significant traffic on the table.


## Common Ground Optimization

While models differ, they share common preferences: clear structure, factual accuracy, authoritative positioning, and fresh content. Optimizing for these universal factors improves performance across all platforms.

The foundational GEO work of structure, entity optimization, and authority building transfers across models even when specific behaviors differ.


## Platform-Specific Adjustments

Beyond common ground, each platform has peculiarities. Some weight Wikipedia more heavily. Some have more recent knowledge cutoffs. Some prefer certain content formats.

Monitor which platforms cite your content and which do not. Investigate patterns in what content performs well on each platform.


## The Perplexity Factor

Perplexity and similar retrieval-augmented models access real-time web content rather than relying solely on training data. For these platforms, traditional SEO signals still matter because they affect retrieval ranking.

A dual strategy that maintains traditional SEO while adding GEO optimization covers both model types.
