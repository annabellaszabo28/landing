Every platform promises results. The reality is that different platforms work for different businesses, and spreading budget too thin across platforms prevents success on any of them. Our November 2025 research provides frameworks for strategic platform selection.


## The Intent Spectrum

Platforms exist on an intent spectrum from high to low. Google Search captures users actively looking for solutions. Instagram captures users in browse mode. Each position on the spectrum requires different creative approaches and expectation calibration.

High-intent platforms typically have higher click costs but higher conversion rates. Low-intent platforms have cheaper reach but require more nurturing.

According to [Meta Business documentation](https://www.facebook.com/business/), average conversion paths from social discovery to purchase span seven or more touchpoints.


## Platform-Audience Fit

Your audience lives on specific platforms. B2B executives are on LinkedIn, not TikTok. Gen Z consumers are on TikTok, not LinkedIn. Platform selection starts with where your audience actually spends time.

Beyond presence, consider context. The same person on LinkedIn is in professional mode. The same person on Instagram is in leisure mode. Match your message to the mindset.


## The Concentration Principle

Budget spread across many platforms means insufficient budget on each. Insufficient budget prevents the testing and optimization needed to find profitable vectors.

Start concentrated on one or two platforms. Prove profitability. Then expand to additional platforms with proven approaches adapted to new contexts.


## Attribution Across Platforms

Multi-platform strategies create attribution challenges. A user who sees a LinkedIn ad, searches on Google, and converts on a retargeting display ad touched three platforms. Which gets credit?

The answer matters for budget allocation. Invest in attribution modeling that captures cross-platform journeys rather than crediting only last click.
