The first quarter creates either momentum or recovery pressure that defines the entire year. Companies that start strong compound their advantage. Companies that start slow spend the rest of the year catching up.

Our December 2025 playbook focuses on Q1 planning that positions you for sustained momentum.


## The 100-Day Sprint Framework

The first hundred days of the year should achieve enough to justify next year's budget. This creates both urgency and focus.

Define three to five outcomes that would make Q1 unambiguously successful. Not activities, not effort, but measurable outcomes that matter to the business.

According to [HBR research](https://hbr.org/), Teams with clear short-term goals outperform teams with only annual goals by thirty eight percent because feedback loops are faster.


## Resource Allocation Reality

Your Q1 resources are your December resources plus any hires that start in January. Assuming new hires will be productive in Q1 is a common planning error.

Build your Q1 plan using only resources that are proven and ready on January first. Any new resources are upside, not baseline.

This constraint forces focus. You cannot do everything, so you must choose what matters most.


## The January Execution Advantage

January offers unique execution advantages: motivated team members returning from rest, customers in planning mode, and lower competitive noise as others recover from holidays.

Companies that execute aggressively in January while competitors are still ramping up capture disproportionate attention. Front-load your major initiatives to January.


## Contingency Planning for Reality

No plan survives contact with reality. Build explicit contingency plans for your top three failure scenarios.

What do you do if your main campaign underperforms? What do you do if a key team member leaves? What do you do if the market shifts unexpectedly? Having answers ready prevents paralysis.
